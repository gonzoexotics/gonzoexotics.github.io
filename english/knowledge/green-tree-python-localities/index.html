Green Tree Pythons

Biak, Aru, Manokwari, Sorong and other names: where they are, what they actually tell us about an animal and why ‘chondro’, Morelia viridis and Morelia azurea are not three interchangeable labels.

KEY CONCLUSION

A locality is geographic information supported by provenance. Colour, pattern or head shape alone is not a locality. Biak, Aru, Manokwari or Sorong may describe a genuine locality line, a locality-type phenotype or merely a former shipping port — these meanings must not be confused.

Appearance can suggest. Pedigree can document. A photograph alone cannot confirm locality.

The most extensive modern analysis of the complex — mitochondrial genomes, 389 nuclear exons and morphology — supports two species separated mainly by New Guinea's central mountain ranges. The authors recognised three subspecies in the northern lineage. This produces four named taxa, not four species.

FACT2 species

M. viridis in the south and M. azurea in the north.

FACT3 northern subspecies

M. a. azurea, M. a. pulcher and M. a. utaraensis.

IMPORTANTlocality ≠ taxon

‘Sorong’ and ‘Cyclops’ are husbandry/geographic names, not separate species.

ONE SNAKE • THREE VOCABULARIES

Chondro, Green Tree Python and Morelia

‘Chondro’ is an established hobby abbreviation derived from the former genus Chondropython. It is a convenient historical hobby term, not a current genus or a third species. Green Tree Python is a common name. Morelia viridis and Morelia azurea are the scientific names of the two lineages currently recognised in The Reptile Database.

HOBBYchondro / GTP

An informal captive-keeping term for the whole group. It does not settle taxonomy.

COMMON NAMEGreen Tree Python

A descriptive name used for both currently recognised species.

SCIENCEMorelia viridis / M. azurea

Taxon names based on diagnoses and a hypothesis of relationships.

Nomenclature does not change biology overnight.

Older books and care sheets may describe animals correctly under the broad name Morelia viridis. The new classification organises their relationships; it does not automatically invalidate observations, but requires checking which population they actually concerned.

FROM ARU TO GENOMICS

150 years of changing names

  1. 1872
    Python viridis

    Hermann Schlegel describes the species from specimens collected on the Aru Islands. Aru remains the type locality of the name viridis.

  2. 1874
    Chondropython azureus

    Adolf Bernhard Meyer describes a specimen from Korido on Biak and establishes the genus Chondropython. This is the origin of the later term ‘chondro’.

  3. 1878
    Chondropython pulcher

    Henri Sauvage describes material from Mansinam Island, around 6 km from present-day Manokwari.

  4. 1893
    Synonymisation

    George Boulenger places the names azureus and pulcher in synonymy with the broadly defined viridis.

  5. 1993
    Morelia viridis

    Kluge's analysis places the Green Tree Python in the genus Morelia.

  6. 2003
    Two deep lineages

    Rawlings and Donnellan identify northern and southern mtDNA lineages differing by an average of 7.6%, but deliberately refrain from formalising a split without broader data.

  7. 2019/20
    Two species, three northern subspecies

    Natusch and colleagues combine mitochondrial data, hundreds of nuclear markers and morphology, proposing the conservative division used in this article.

  8. 2026
    Current lists

    The Reptile Database recognises M. viridis and M. azurea; it lists three subspecies within the latter species.

GEOGRAPHY BEFORE COLOUR

Key localities — what we can honestly say

ARU ISLANDSM. viridis

Aru

The type locality of viridis, south-west of New Guinea. Field and captive data support yellow young only. Adults often have white ‘rosettes’ along the back, but their absence does not exclude Aru origin.

Pitfall:

a white pattern is not a certificate of origin.

CENDERAWASIH BAYM. a. azurea

Biak

An isolated island population. It can produce red and yellow young; ontogenetic colour change may be late, and adult phenotype is exceptionally variable. In morphological analysis, a long, dark-tipped tail was often distinctive.

Pitfall:

late colour change and reactivity are tendencies, not tests of purity.

VOGELKOP / BIRD'S HEADM. a. pulcher

Manokwari

Mansinam near Manokwari is the type locality of the name pulcher. Scientific material usually describes uniform lime green with a pale-blue pattern, but characters overlap strongly with other northern populations.

Pitfall:

‘Manokwari type’ is more honest when the pedigree does not reach documented founders.

WESTERN VOGELKOPM. a. pulcher

Sorong

Geographically, a genuine population from the Sorong area lies in the western range of pulcher. Sorong is also an important centre and historical trade hub.

Pitfall:

the place of shipment or purchase need not be where the ancestors were collected.

RAJA AMPATM. a. pulcher

Kofiau, Misool, Salawati, Waigeo

Under the proposed arrangement, the western islands belong to pulcher. A broad study found no red young in Raja Ampat samples, although they occur on nearby Vogelkop.

Pitfall:

‘Raja Ampat’ encompasses different islands and should not replace a specific locality.

NORTHERN NEW GUINEAM. a. utaraensis

Jayapura / Cyclops

Hobby labels from around Jayapura and the Cyclops Mountains fall within the northern mainland lineage. Red and yellow young are possible, and a pale-blue adult pattern is a population tendency.

Pitfall:

a city, a mountain range and a captive line represent three different levels of precision.

CENDERAWASIH BAYM. a. utaraensis

Yapen

Although close to Biak, Yapen was not included in the subspecies azurea. In the systematic analysis, it belongs to the northern mainland lineage utaraensis.

Pitfall:

island proximity does not automatically mean the same evolutionary history.

SOUTHERN NEW GUINEAM. viridis

Merauke

A southern locality belonging to M. viridis. Genetic data link southern New Guinea, Aru and Australia's Cape York into one southern lineage.

Pitfall:

a single snake's large size does not confirm a Merauke label.

A NAME REQUIRES DATAdo not assign automatically

Wamena / ‘highland’

The central mountains are the main barrier between lineages, but not a simple line. Research has identified exceptions and populations in high valleys on both sides of the divide.

Conclusion:

without a precise location and pedigree, a broad highland label is insufficient for assigning a taxon.

PHENOTYPE IS NOT A PASSPORT

Can locality be identified by appearance?

The shortest answer is: not with the reliability needed to confirm origin. Natusch's team identified only one particularly useful general difference for an inexperienced observer: red young occur in M. azurea and have not been confirmed in M. viridis. However, yellow young occur in both species, so yellow colour alone settles nothing.

Even the full morphological analysis used many characters together: scale counts, tail proportions, iris pattern, colour-change rate and dorsal pattern arrangement. The authors themselves emphasised overlap, particularly between pulcher and utaraensis.

MAY SUPPORT AN ASSESSMENTa combination of characters

Juvenile colour, tail, dorsal pattern, iris and scalation — considered together.

INSUFFICIENTone adult photograph

Selective breeding, locality crosses and natural variation may mimic a ‘type’.

STRONGEST EVIDENCEan unbroken pedigree

Parent, breeder and founder data matter more than a marketing description.

PRACTITIONERS' VIEW

Experienced breeders and science speak the same language here

Greg Maxwell drew attention for years to the myth of identifying locality by appearance. GS Reptiles devoted a separate piece to the topic, and the archived Kingsnake gallery warns that some names derive from shipping ports and old stocks of uncertain origin. The practical principle is consistent: without documentation, use ‘locality type’ rather than claiming a pure locality.

A SNAKE IN THE LANDSCAPE

Where and how Green Tree Pythons actually live

The complex occupies a highly varied area: lowland rainforests, secondary forests, regenerating edges, village gardens and some foothill habitats. It occurs from sea level to at least around 2000 m. There is therefore no single ‘chondro climate’ with one temperature and humidity for Biak, Aru and New Guinea's high valleys.

MICROHABITATJuveniles lower down, adults more often in closed forest

Ontogenetic colour change coincides with changes in diet, head shape and habitat use. Juveniles studied in the wild fed mainly on small skinks.

STRATEGYAmbush hunting does not mean lifelong immobility

A python may use one hunting position for many days and then move elsewhere.

MOVEMENTTelemetry challenges the ‘one tree’ myth

In an Australian population of 27 tracked individuals, adult females had stable home ranges averaging 6.21 ± 1.85 ha; males and juveniles did not show equally stable home ranges.

CAUTIONOne study does not describe every locality

The result from Australian M. viridis demonstrates movement capacity, but does not prove identical ecology in every subspecies of M. azurea.

A MYTH IN POPULAR MATERIALS

‘A chondro may spend its entire life within a radius of a few dozen metres.’

This cannot be used as a general truth about the complex. Remaining coiled on a perch for hours is normal, but radio tracking has revealed varied and seasonal movements. An arboreal ambush predator is not biologically ‘immobile’.

PEDIGREE • TRADE • CONSERVATION

A locality has value only when it can be traced

A trade study published in 2011 documented thousands of wild-caught pythons in the Indonesian supply chain subsequently declared as captive-bred. These are historical data, not evidence that every current import has that origin. They nevertheless show why an undocumented seller's label cannot replace pedigree.

01

Ask for the full name

Species or subspecies, locality or an honest ‘locality type’, and both origins for a cross.

02

Trace backwards

Animal IDs, parents, the breeder of each generation and the founding pair are more useful than a photograph.

03

Distinguish port from collection site

Sorong or Manokwari may denote a region, city or trading point. Documentation should explain exactly what the label means.

04

Do not fill gaps with assumptions

If continuity is broken, label the animal as locality type or origin unknown. This strengthens, rather than weakens, a breeder's credibility.

Science is developing independent provenance verification.

A pilot study in 2025 found that strontium isotopes in shed skins may help challenge false geographic claims. The method needs larger samples and local reference values, however; it is not currently a home ‘locality test’.

SHORT ANSWERS

Frequently asked questions

Are chondro and Morelia viridis the same thing?

‘Chondro’ is a historical hobby term used for the whole complex. Under current systematics, the complex includes M. viridis and M. azurea, so the term is broader than one modern species.

Is Biak still Morelia viridis?

In older literature, yes, in the broad sense. Under Natusch and colleagues' arrangement and the current Reptile Database, the Biak population is Morelia azurea azurea.

Are Sorong or Manokwari separate subspecies?

No. If the origin is genuine, they fall within the geographic range of M. a. pulcher. Their names remain useful locality labels in captivity.

Do red young indicate the northern lineage?

Yes — the available broad studies found no red young in M. viridis. The reverse does not apply: yellow young also occur in all subspecies of M. azurea.

Can a photograph distinguish Aru from Biak?

It can indicate resemblance to a typical phenotype, but cannot confirm origin. Animals vary, and locality crosses and selected lines exist in captivity.

Does every locality need different husbandry parameters?

Geography and elevation affect climate, so origin provides important context. However, do not copy averages from the nearest weather station into an enclosure. Stability, a gradient, hydration, ventilation and observation of the individual remain more important than a ‘programme for a name’.

THE GONZO EXOTICS STANDARD

The most honest label is the best label

If you know the origin, document it. If you know only the type, call it a type. If you do not know, do not guess. This protects genuine locality lines, avoids misleading buyers and leaves science with usable data.
