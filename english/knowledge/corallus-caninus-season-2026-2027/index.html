THE JOURNAL'S PRINCIPLE

This series will proceed chronologically. FACT means a change or event already completed and recorded. OBSERVATION describes what was actually noticed. PLAN is the next step, rather than a statement that it has already happened. CONCLUSION remains a working interpretation that can be revised as further data emerge.

01
The animal's welfare comes before the seasonal calendar, and the season comes before expectations of a breeding result.

There is no claim here that the current actions will ‘trigger’ breeding. In Corallus caninus the response depends on health, condition, age, individual history and the whole environment. This entry documents the first preparation phase; subsequent parts will be written only after actual observations have been collected.

WORKING CONCLUSION

A change of rhythm, rather than a ‘strong stimulus’

The most likely function of the adjustments so far is a gradual transition away from the summer rhythm: shorter light exposure, cooler nights and maintenance of the evening water cycle. I do not treat any single number as an independent target. Day, night, misting, ventilation, drying rate, condition and behaviour form one system.

01Condition first

No deterioration in welfare is a prerequisite for further changes.

02Small steps

The photoperiod and night temperatures are reduced in stages so the response can be assessed.

03No automatic conclusions

Changes in position, appetite or activity can have many causes.

This is not a universal protocol for every C. caninus. In particular, we do not automatically transfer experience with Corallus batesii (Amazon Basin) to Northern Corallus caninus. Species differences and differences between installations matter more than the apparent precision of a table.

WHAT WILL BE RECORDED NEXT

More than temperature and time

Subsequent entries will distinguish routine notes from events important to the season. No new signs or no change is also valuable information — gaps will not be filled with speculation.

Limits of responsibility

Suspended feeding and environmental changes apply only to the current project, guided by the condition of the individual animals. Unusual signs, deteriorating general condition or breathing problems require assessment by a reptile veterinarian, rather than continuing the plan ‘for the calendar’.

PRACTICE AND SOURCES

What comparison of breeding accounts shows

FACT FROM THE SOURCES: published Emerald Tree Boa recommendations differ in detail, and some older material predates the renewed separation of C. caninus and C. batesii. Numbers and breeding accounts from one collection therefore cannot automatically become a recipe for every animal.

COMMON GROUND

  • Breeders describe seasonality as a sequence of changes, rather than one switch.
  • Ventilation, water access and hygiene are assessed alongside humidity.
  • Adult welfare and condition come before breeding decisions.
  • Check the source material's taxon and provenance before transferring a conclusion.

CONCLUSION FOR THIS JOURNAL: the Gonzo Exotics schedule is an individual, cautious observation programme. It is intended to be transparent and assessable over time, but is not a set of instructions to copy without knowing your own animals and conditions.
