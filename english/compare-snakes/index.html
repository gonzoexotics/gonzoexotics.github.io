01 • Pantherophis guttatus

Corn snake

Adult size
usually 100–150 cm
Enclosure plan
approx. 120 × 60 × 60 cm
Microclimate
moderate gradient and access to water

A good reference point for a thoughtfully prepared first enclosure: the species is slender, mobile and usually does not require a tropical microclimate. That doesn't mean it can live in a random box or without controlled heating.

Worth knowing: Corn snakes are adept at escaping. Secure enclosures, hiding places and calm acclimation to human presence matter more than the label "easy".

02 • Python regius

Ball python

Adult size
usually 90–140 cm
Enclosure plan
approx. 120 × 60 × 60 cm
Microclimate
stable gradient, ventilation and shedding control

The popularity of the ball python does not remove the need for preparation. It is primarily a species to observe and often responds well to calm, limited handling after acclimation, but microclimate and feeding mistakes can quietly accumulate.

For whom: for someone who wants to work systematically and does not expect an animal that will automatically feed and always welcome contact.

03 • Boa imperator

Boa imperator

Adult size
approximately 180–260 cm
Enclosure plan
approx. 180 × 80 × 80 cm
Microclimate
thermal gradient and surfaces that have time to dry

This is a large constrictor, though not a giant species. It needs a durable enclosure, larger prey and a plan for when it reaches full adult mass. Even a calm individual must be lifted using proper technique and with attention to its reactions.

Decision limit: the choice only makes sense if the eventual space is available now, not at some unspecified future time.

04 • Boa constrictor

Boa constrictor

Adult size
approximately 220–300 cm
Enclosure plan
approx. 240 × 100 × 100 cm
Microclimate
stable gradient and ventilation without drying out

In this case, the difference from the boa imperator is not cosmetic. Greater length and mass affect enclosure construction, transport, cleaning, prey size and safe handling procedures.

Do not choose this species, if it is primarily a young animal that fascinates you. The plan must include an adult boa for two or three decades.

05 • Heterodon nasicus

Western hognose snake

Adult size
approximately 45–90 cm
Enclosure plan
approximately 100 × 50 × 40 cm
Microclimate
drier gradient and local hiding place moist

Small size does not mean "plush" character. Heterodon is active during the day, digs and can display spectacular defensive behavior. This is part of the biology of the species, not evidence of aggression or a reason to force contact.

Best for: a person who wants to observe natural behavior and accepts that not every snake is an animal to be carried around often.

06 • Lampropeltis californiae

California kingsnake

Adult size
approximately 90–150 cm
Enclosure plan
approx. 120 × 60 × 60 cm
Microclimate
moderate, with a humid hide during shedding

This is an active colubrid for a keeper who enjoys a livelier snake. The feeding reaction can be vigorous, so feeding and handling must be clearly separated and the terrarium must be well secured.

Important: it is not kept with other snakes. The "king" in the name does not mean a species for a shared terrarium.

07 • Antaresia maculosa

Spotted python

Adult size
approximately 90–140 cm
Enclosure plan
approx. 120 × 60 × 60 cm
Microclimate
controlled gradient, moderate humidity

A compact python is not a decoration. It needs secure hiding places and the ability to thermoregulate; its reactions to conditions should be observed. Compared with tropical arboreal species, the microclimate may be simpler, but it must still be measured rather than guessed.

Practically: availability is lower than in the ball python, so the animal's source and feeding history are important.

08 • Morelia spilota

Carpet python

Adult size
approximately 180–260 cm
Enclosure plan
approx. 180 × 80 × 100 cm
Microclimate
gradient and use of height

The "carpet python" includes animals of different lines and origins, so it is not worth copying one pattern without asking about a specific individual. This is a larger, active species that uses vertical space, and the young may be noticeably reactive.

Decision: suitable for a person who already has room for an adult, larger prey and understands the difference between activity and a "bad character".

09 • Morelia viridis

Green tree python

Adult size
approximately 120–180 cm
Enclosure plan
approx. 120 × 60 × 90 cm
Microclimate
precise gradient, humidity cycles and critical ventilation

This is an arboreal species for observation, not for regular removal. The quality of the perches, the arrangement of the zones, the absence of overheating and the ability of the terrarium to dry after misting are more important than the decorative effect.

Do not automatically compare it with Corallus caninus. A similar silhouette on the perch does not mean identical biology or an identical care model. Full Morelia viridis guide →

10 • Corallus caninus

Northern emerald tree boa

Adult size
approximately 150–220 cm
Enclosure plan
approx. 120 × 60 × 120 cm
Microclimate
dynamic humidity, hydration, drying out and critical ventilation

The Northern emerald tree boa requires consistency, patience and an efficient microclimate system. The goal is not to maintain the same humidity year-round or keep the surface wet 24 hours a day. What matters is the relationship between spraying, water, air circulation, time of day and drying time.

Important distinction: data for Corallus batesii are not automatically instructions for C. caninus. Full Corallus caninus guide →

11 • Python bivittatus

Burmese python

Adult size
approximately 350–550 cm
Enclosure plan
a custom enclosure or room
Microclimate
large, stable gradient and very good ventilation

This is not a "larger ball python". An adult’s mass changes the logistics of feeding, transport, cleaning, heating failures and safe handling. Even a calm animal does not remove the need for procedures and another person’s support when control is required.

Entry condition: space, budget, large prey and a safety plan must be in place before purchase. Full Python bivittatus guide → (PL)

12 • Epicrates cenchria

Brazilian rainbow boa

Adult size
approximately 150–200 cm
Enclosure plan
approximately 150 × 70 × 70 cm
Microclimate
higher humidity, but without a constantly wet substrate

The impressive appearance easily distracts attention from the microclimate. The rainbow boa is not a "dry boa" and keeping the ground constantly wet does not solve the problem - humidity control, ventilation and space to choose the conditions are needed.

For whom: for a caregiver ready to constantly monitor the system, not just the reading from one hygrometer.

13 • Gonyosoma oxycephalum

Red-tailed green ratsnake

Adult size
approximately 180–240 cm
Enclosure plan
approx. 180 × 80 × 180 cm
Microclimate
tropical gradient, vertical and constant air circulation

A large, fast arboreal species for a person who values ​​observation and can work without forcing contact. The height of the terrarium is not an addition: it determines the arrangement of the perches, the selection of zones and the possibility of movement.

Not for everyone: the speed and reactivity do not match the expectation of a calm, frequently handled snake.

14 • Pituophis catenifer

Gopher snake

Adult size
approximately 120–210 cm
Enclosure plan
approx. 180 × 70 × 60 cm
Microclimate
moderate gradient and local moist hiding place

Active colubrid with expressive defensive behavior. Loud hissing and defensive displays may appear threatening, but they require calm, predictable behavior rather than punishing the animal for a natural reaction.

Best for: a person who is comfortable with an active, larger snake and who doesn't expect the temperament to always be "easy."

15 • Eryx colubrinus

Kenyan sand boa

Adult size
approximately 45–80 cm
Enclosure plan
approx. 90 × 45 × 40 cm
Microclimate
drier substrate, gradient and constant access to water

The compact, digging boa allows you to observe a different lifestyle than species that actively move in plain view. A deep, adequate substrate is not decoration: it enables normal behavior.

Fair Expectation: part of this species' life will be invisible. If you care primarily about an animal that is constantly exposed, choose a different care model.

16 • Lampropeltis triangulum

Milk snake

Adult size
usually 60–120 cm
Enclosure plan
approx. 120 × 60 × 60 cm
Microclimate
moderate gradient, water and a hide during shedding

Under the name "milk snake", trade and breeding can combine animals of different origins, so before purchasing you need to know the exact form, feeding history and expected size. This is an active colubrid, not just a striking pattern.

What to really compare: terrarium security, feeding response and scale of the adult animal - not just the price or color of the juvenile.

17 • Pantherophis obsoletus

Western ratsnake

Adult size
approximately 120–180 cm
Enclosure plan
approximately 150 × 70 × 80 cm
Microclimate
moderate gradient and room to move

This is a clearly larger and more active rat snake than a typical corn snake. It uses the ground and stable branches, so the height and structure of the interior should not be an addition left at the end of the project.

The most important difference: a longer terrarium, greater ability to escape and the animal's pace require a more conscious daily routine.

18 • Elaphe schrenckii

Amur ratsnake

Adult size
approximately 120–180 cm
Enclosure plan
approximately 150 × 70 × 80 cm
Microclimate
moderate, with a well-thought-out annual cycle

The Amur ratsnake is an active species from temperate zones. The model of a year-round, highly tropical terrarium should not be transferred to it; husbandry must be matched to the individual’s origin and the keeper’s goals.

For whom: for a person who likes an active, larger colubrid and can plan conditions in advance, instead of looking for one universal setting.

19 • Boaedon fuliginosus

African house snake

Adult size
approximately 60–110 cm
Enclosure plan
approximately 100 × 50 × 50 cm
Microclimate
moderate gradient, hiding places and ventilation

It is a small, mainly crepuscular and nocturnal African snake. It can remain unobtrusive during the day, so its value is not in constant exposure, but in observing calm, natural behavior after dark.

Important before purchasing: "mahogany" is sometimes a trade name. The identification and documentation of a specific animal is more important than the tag itself in the ad.

20 • Eunectes notaeus

Yellow anaconda

Adult size
approximately 250–400 cm
Enclosure plan
a large, individually designed semi-aquatic enclosure
Microclimate
clean water, a land area that can dry, and strong ventilation

This is a species that cannot be honestly described by a single "minimal terrarium." The water must be designed for hygiene and safe service, and the land part must be designed for the actual weight of an adult animal. An ordinary tank with a swimming pool does not solve these problems.

Infrastructure decision: before an animal arrives, the final enclosure, drainage, cleaning method, emergency plan and safe work rules must already be in place.

21 • Eunectes murinus

Green anaconda

Adult size
approximately 350–500 cm
Enclosure plan
a custom-designed room or enclosure
Microclimate
large water body, drying of land and critical ventilation

The green anaconda requires infrastructure similar to a small breeding project, not a classic home terrarium. Its mass affects every stage of care: feeding, transport, changing water, cleaning and the need to work with another person.

Limits of responsibility: the entry "calm" in the description of a specific individual does not reduce the risk resulting from its final weight and strength.

22 • Malayopython reticulatus

Reticulated python

Adult size
approximately 350–600 cm
Enclosure plan
a large enclosure or room for an adult
Microclimate
stable gradient, drying surfaces and strong ventilation

A reticulated python is not simply a larger ball python. It is fast, very strong and can reach a size that completely changes everyday logistics. Differences between lines do not remove the need to plan for a large, heavy adult.

Entry condition: space, a source of large prey, a budget, help from another person and safety procedures must be ready before purchase, not after the first year of growth.

23 • Boiga dendrophila

Mangrove cat snake

Adult size
approximately 150–220 cm
Enclosure plan
approximately 150 × 80 × 180 cm, specially secured
Microclimate
tropical gradient, vertical, drying and critical ventilation

Boiga is the name of the genus, so this comparison specifically refers to the mangrove cat snake Boiga dendrophila. It is a fast, nocturnal, arboreal, venomous species with rear venomous teeth - information about it should not be treated as a purchasing instruction.

Safety over appearance: This species is not for beginners or for routine handling. Up-to-date legal verification, secure containment, and a viable response plan in the event of a bite are necessary before considering maintenance.

How to use profiles: first eliminate species for which you don't have the target space, budget or willingness to be responsible. Only then compare appearance, availability and personal preferences. BEFORE YOU COMPARE

A good choice doesn't start with a difficulty ranking

The comparator shows the differences in one place, but the result is worth reading as a list of topics to check. There is no species suitable for everyone, and a simple label of easy or difficult does not describe the entire care system.

Scale and security

Check the target size, strength of the animal, transport, cleaning and safe handling rules. The plan must work for the adult, not just the young.

Terrarium as a system

Dimensions are just the beginning. What counts are stable perches or hiding places, temperature gradient, ventilation, ability to control parameters and access to cleaning.

Source and documentation

Before purchasing, ask for a real feeding history, information about its origin, current video material and documents appropriate to the species and country.

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING

The comparison tool is supposed to narrow down the selection, not close the topic

Does similar appearance mean similar conditions?

NO. Compare the biology, size, activity, thermoregulation and real availability of healthy animals from a documented source. In particular, parameters should not be transferred between separate species just because they look similar.

What is more important than the purchase price itself?

The fixed cost of infrastructure, provision for diagnostics and treatment, the size of an adult animal, the logistics of care and the ability to provide an appropriate terrarium throughout the animal's life.

When to postpone the purchase?

When the terrarium is not yet functioning stably, there is no quarantine plan or veterinary assistance, and the choice is based primarily on color, price or the "hassle-free" claim.
